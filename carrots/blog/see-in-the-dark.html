Will Carrots Help You See in the Dark?

At least once a week someone at the stall tells their kid to eat their carrots so they can see in the dark. I always want to say something. So here it is, written down, so I can point at it.

Where the idea came from

The story most people tell goes back to the Second World War. Britain's air force had started using radar to find enemy planes at night and didn't want anyone to know. So, the story goes, they let it be known that their best night pilots were eating a lot of carrots. One of them, John Cunningham, got the nickname "Cat's Eyes" in the papers.

At the same time the government was pushing carrots at home, because they were easy to grow and there was plenty of them when other food was rationed. Posters, recipes and cartoon characters all told people that carrots would help them get around during the blackout.

Historians still argue about how much of this was a deliberate cover story and how much was just enthusiastic vegetable marketing. Either way, the idea stuck, and eighty-odd years later it's still being said at my stall.

Carrots won't give you night vision. But not having enough vitamin A can take some of yours away.

What a carrot actually does

There is a grain of truth in it. Orange carrots are full of beta-carotene, which your body turns into vitamin A. Your eyes need vitamin A to make the pigment that lets you see in low light. People who don't get enough of it can develop night blindness, and in parts of the world where diets are short on it, that's a real problem.

But if you already eat reasonably well, eating extra carrots won't push your eyesight past normal. You can't top up your way to superpowers. I've eaten more carrots than anyone I know, and I still walk into the shed door in the dark.

Carrots weren't always orange

The other thing people don't expect: the earliest cultivated carrots were mostly purple and yellow. Orange carrots became common a few hundred years ago, largely thanks to growers in the Netherlands. You'll hear that they bred them orange in honour of William of Orange. It's a good story, but there's not much evidence for it. They were probably just tastier and more reliable.

We grow a few purple and yellow ones each season. They're a bit earthier than the orange ones and look great roasted whole. Ask at the stall if you want some mixed in with your order.

So should your kids eat their carrots?

Yes. Not because they'll see in the dark, but because carrots are cheap, they keep for weeks, they're good for you, and a Big carrot straight out of the ground is one of the best things you'll ever eat. That should be enough.
